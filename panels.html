The ten panels

The rail on the left holds ten panels. Each one answers a different question about the realm. Hovering any rail button tells you which — this page says it at length, with every control named.

The panels

  1. Roster — who is here
  2. Feelings — who likes whom
  3. Groups — who is together
  4. Companies — guilds and land
  5. Chronicle — the written record
  6. Market — the auction houses
  7. Commands — pause and resume
  8. Lore — write your own
  9. Memories — what they carry
  10. Journey — one life in order

Two words worth pinning down. A company is a guild — a standing organisation with seats, land and rivalries. A group is a party — a handful of characters standing together right now. They have separate panels because they are separate things.

Roster

Everyone in the world right now

The census, and the fastest way to find one person. Each row gives a character's name, their level, class and where they are standing, with a coloured dot for what they are doing.

Controls

  • Find by name — the search box. Press / anywhere in the dashboard to jump into it.
  • Notable — real players, paused bots, and anyone inside an instance. The default, and usually the right one.
  • On this map — everyone on the continent you are looking at.
  • In combat — everyone fighting at this instant.
  • Dead — everyone awaiting a resurrection.
  • Everyone — all nine hundred-odd, alphabetically.

Each chip carries its own live count, so the filter row doubles as a summary of the realm. Lists cap at 150 rows and tell you how many more there are; narrow the search rather than scrolling.

Clicking a row opens that character in the inspector, switches the map to their continent, and pans to them.

Feelings

Who has warmed to or cooled on whom

The social life of the realm. Characters form opinions by talking, by overhearing others, and by what happens to them — and every shift is recorded with the reason for it.

The four sections

SectionWhat is in it
Recent momentsThe latest shifts: X warmed to Y or X cooled on Y, how much, and why. The timestamp's tooltip says whether it came from chat, from being overheard, or from an event.
OverheardWhole conversations. Open one and you get the exchange as it was spoken, then the feelings it caused in whoever was listening.
Warmest tiesThe strongest friendships in the world, with a phrase describing each and how many moments built it.
Coldest tiesThe same, for the people who cannot stand each other.

A section heading reading 25 of 14,494 means you are seeing the top of a much larger pile. The small expand button on each heading — Show every one, full screen — opens the whole list; see Feelings in full.

Every name is a link. Click one to open that character.

Groups

Who is standing together right now, and what they are saying

Characters find each other and travel together without being told to. This panel lists every group currently formed, who is in it, where they are, how long they have been together and how much they have said.

The line above the list sums it up — how many groups, how many bots, how many real players, and how many are talking. A row reading 266 yd apart means the group has drifted; one reading apart means its members are in different zones entirely.

Clicking a row opens that group full screen, on its own map, with its whole conversation. The Open Groups button opens the wall of every group at once — see the group wall.

Companies

Seats, lands, feuds and who is joining

Companies are the realm's guilds. They hold territory, keep seats of power, court new members and fall out with one another.

The four sections

  • Companies — every company, under Alliance and Horde headings, with what it holds and contests.
  • Candidacies — who is trying to join whom, and how far along they are: noticed, spoken for, offered a place, sworn in. Sponsors and opponents are named.
  • Fallen companies — collapsed by default. Companies that broke apart, went quiet, or left remnants.
  • Recent incidents — what has happened between companies lately: land Claimed, Taken, Held, Contested, or two of them hunting the same prey.

Clicking a company opens it in the inspector — its seats, its lands, and what it says about each rival. Its territory lights up on the map at the same time.

Chronicle

Each faction scribe's watches and the word going around

Each faction has a scribe who writes up the world's events in period prose, one watch at a time, from what actually happened. Rumours travel outward from where they started and distort as they go.

Controls

  • Alliance / Horde — whose scribe you are reading. Each faction sees the world differently.
  • Open Chronicle — the full-screen reader, laid out as a newspaper.
  • Your Chronicle — an edition covering only what your own characters took part in.

Inside a watch, Word going around collects the rumours. Labels like Carried on, Further still and Far and wide tell you how many hands a story has passed through — the further it has travelled, the less reliable it is.

Market

The auction houses: stalls, sales and what goods fetch

Merchants stock the auction houses, ordinary characters list what they loot, and the market works out what things are actually worth.

  • Auction houses — one bar per house, split by who is selling: merchant, townsfolk or player.
  • Last day — twenty-four hours of sold, listed and expired. Hover a column for that hour's numbers.
  • Most traded goods — item names in their quality colours, with the price the market has learned and an arrow showing which way it moved.
  • Recent sales — what changed hands, for how much, and between whom. Anything involving a real player is highlighted.

The market file is rewritten every ten minutes. If it falls behind, a stale badge appears beside the heading.

Commands

Pause or resume a bot on the world server

The only panel that changes the world rather than reporting on it, and the only one that needs a password.

Pausing freezes a character where they stand. They will still defend themselves if attacked, and the pause ends when they log out or the server restarts — it is a way to hold someone still, not to remove them.

  1. Paste your server's command token into the token field. It is kept in your browser only, and never leaves it except as a header on the command itself.
  2. Select a character — from the map, the roster, anywhere.
  3. Press Pause in the inspector. It becomes Resume.

Try to pause someone without a token and the dashboard brings you to this panel and asks for it. Every result the server sends back is listed under Recent results, so you can see what took and what didn't.

Where the token comes from. It is the Dashboard.CommandToken setting on the server. If it was never set, the panel simply reads Commands are off and the pause buttons do nothing.

Lore

Write your characters' story, and let the world check it

This is where you write yourself into the world. Your own characters can be given a backstory, a turn of speech, and a relationship to each other — and the world checks what you wrote before it accepts it.

How it goes

  1. Name your main. The character you actually play as yourself. The rest become their alts, and can be bound to them.
  2. Pick a character from Your characters. What already stands for them appears below.
  3. Bring them into the world. Choose what is between them and your main — only ties that pair could genuinely have are offered — plus a turn of speech and a few words of your own. Surprise me fills all three in as suggestions you can edit.
  4. Write it, read what came back, and Bring them in when you are happy. Nothing is stored until you do.

Under Words that must survive you can name things the writing must not lose — a cousin, a debt, a home town. If the result drops them, it is written again without them.

The gate

Anything you write is checked against four rules before the world will hold it:

  • Write as though it is real life. No games, no figures.
  • Nothing later than this age has happened yet.
  • Keep to your own people's lands and history.
  • Your own words are kept — the shaping must not lose what you said.

The verdict comes back as This cannot stand in the world, Worth considering, or As the world would tell it. This panel needs its own token, separate from the command token, entered at the bottom under The gate.

Memories

What the bots still carry: their deeds and what they made of them

Not everything that happens is worth remembering. What is, gets written down in the character's own words and weighed out of ten — and they carry it afterwards.

The counts at the top give the total, how many characters are carrying anything, how many were formed today, and the average weight. A memory weighed 10/10 was a defining event; a 3/10 was a Tuesday.

The expand button opens every memory in the world, searchable. To read one character's memories instead, select them and open the Memories tab in the inspector.

Journey

Everything one character has done, in the order it happened

The deepest view in the dashboard: one character's entire life, assembled from the ledger — every kill, quest, level, item, journey, conversation, feeling and memory, in order.

Unlike every other panel, this one lists characters who are offline too. Their record does not stop existing because they logged out.

Search by name, or click any row to open that journey full screen. The journeys are rebuilt every ten minutes. See a character's journey for what is inside one.
