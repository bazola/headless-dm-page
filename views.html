The deep end

Full-screen views

The side panels show you the top of each pile. Behind each one is the whole archive — tens of thousands of rows, searchable, going back to the realm's first day. These five views are where the dashboard stops summarising.

What they have in common

  • All five fill the screen and close with Esc or the × in the corner.
  • All five have their own web address, so you can bookmark one or open it in a tab of its own with the Open in a new tab button.
  • Lists load more as you scroll, with a Show 120 more button at the foot and a line telling you how many are still below. They end with That is all 12,433. so you know you have reached the bottom.
  • Clicking a name closes the view and opens that character in the inspector.

Feelings in full

#moments · #overheard · #warmest · #coldest

Every opinion anyone has ever formed, and every conversation that was overheard forming it.

Four tabs across the top:

TabWhat it lists
MomentsEvery shift in feeling, ever, with its reason and how far it moved.
OverheardEvery conversation, as spoken, with the feelings it caused in whoever heard it.
WarmestEvery tie in the world, ranked from the strongest friendship down.
ColdestThe same list from the other end.

The search box changes with the tab — on Moments it searches names and reasons, on Overheard it searches names, places and anything that was said. Searching for a word someone used is often the fastest way to find the conversation you half-remember.

The group wall

#groups

Every group in the world at once, as cards, each carrying the conversation its members are having.

The talk on each card is split in two. earlier is what these characters said in other company — dimmed, because it is not this group's conversation. since they joined up is what they have said to each other. A card reading Nothing said since they joined up. is a group that has not spoken yet.

Clicking a card opens that group on its own map: every member as a large marker, everyone else on the continent as small grey dots, and the whole conversation beneath. Clicking a marker there jumps you straight to that character in the dashboard. Esc steps back to the wall, then closes it.

Every memory

#memories

Everything every character in the world still carries, heaviest first.

The weight beside each — 10/10, 9/10 — is how much it mattered to them. The heaviest memories are deaths, near-misses and firsts; the lightest are errands.

Search by a person, a place, or a word that would have been in it. Silithus finds everyone who remembers the desert; a character's name finds everything anyone remembers about them, not just their own memories.

A character's journey

#journey/<id>

One life, in the order it happened. This is the closest thing the realm has to a biography.

Reading it

  • The counters — entries, deeds, conversations, feelings, memories, and how many different people they have travelled with.
  • The ribbon — activity across the whole span, as bars. Drag across it to read only that stretch of time; click once to clear.
  • Chapters — the timeline breaks itself into days, and within a day into places, with the companions they were with.

Filtering

The chips across the top — Deeds, Talk, Feelings, Memories, Group, Encounters, Fighting — each turn a kind of entry off. Turning everything off but Talk gives you a transcript of every word they have spoken; leaving only Deeds gives a plain campaign log.

The button at the top left, showing the current character's name, switches to anyone else's journey without leaving the view. Names inside the record are links too — clicking a companion opens their journey, which is how you follow a friendship from both sides.

The chronicle reader

#chronicle · #chronicle-yours

The faction chronicles, set as a newspaper.

  • Alliance / Horde / Yours — whose edition you are reading. Yours covers only what your own characters took part in.
  • The day dropdown, with arrows either side — every edition the realm has produced. ← and → turn the day while the reader is open.

Each article carries its watch, a headline, the scribe's byline, the prose, and a sidebar of Word going around — the rumours attached to that watch. The two factions' editions of the same evening are worth reading side by side: they describe the same events and disagree about them.
